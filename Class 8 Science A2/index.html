Concept 1 • Necessary Evil

Advantages & Disadvantages of Friction

Friction is called a "Necessary Evil" because while it wastes energy and causes wear & tear, life would be impossible without it (walking, writing, braking)!

Advantages (Why We Need It)

Essential

Enables Walking & Grip

Prevents our shoes from slipping on the ground while taking steps.

Writing & Holding Objects

Allows pen nibs to deposit ink on paper and chalk on blackboards.

Braking & Stopping Vehicles

Brake pads rub against wheels to bring moving vehicles to a quick stop.

Disadvantages (The Downsides)

Loss & Damage

Heat Generation & Energy Loss

Converts useful kinetic energy into unwanted heat inside engines and machinery.

Wear and Tear of Parts

Worsens machine gears, damages shoe soles, and wears down car tires over time.

Reduces Mechanical Efficiency

Requires motors to work much harder to overcome surface resistance.

Concept 2 • Surface Modification

Methods to Increase & Decrease Friction

We can control friction depending on our needs! Treaded tires and powder increase grip, while lubricants, ball bearings, and air cushions reduce it.

Ways to INCREASE Friction

  • Grooving / Treading: Treaded tires and spiked shoes (cleats) bite into surfaces for better traction.
  • Coarsening Surfaces: Gymnasts & Kabaddi players apply coarse powder/sand to hands for firm grip.
  • Applying Force: Pressing brake pads tighter against bicycle rims increases friction to stop fast.

Ways to DECREASE Friction

  • Lubrication: Applying oil, grease, or graphite creates a thin layer between surfaces so micro-bumps don't interlock.
  • Ball Bearings: Replaces harsh sliding friction with smooth rolling friction in wheels and motors.
  • Fine Powder / Air Cushion: Talcum powder on Carrom boards fills micro-pits, making strikers glide effortlessly!

Concept 3 • Fluid Mechanics

Fluid Friction (Drag) & Streamlining

Fluids (liquids and gases) exert a friction force called DRAG on objects moving through them!

1. What is Fluid Drag?

The resistive force exerted by water or air opposing an object moving through it.

2. Direction of Drag

Drag always acts opposite to the direction of motion relative to the fluid.

3. How to Reduce Drag?

By giving objects a STREAMLINED shape (tapered ends, smooth body like birds, fish, aeroplanes).
